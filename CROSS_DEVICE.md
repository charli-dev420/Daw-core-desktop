## One project model, two platforms

DAWWW-CORE treats Desktop and Android as two places to work on the **same project**, not as a full DAW plus a reduced mobile companion.

The portable `.dw` project keeps the creative session together: arrangement, patterns and notes, instrument state, effect settings, automation, routing and referenced sampler content remain part of the same project when it moves between platforms.

```text
Desktop  →  .dw  →  Android  →  .dw  →  Desktop
```

There is no mobile-only project format and no conversion step between the two surfaces.

## Why `.dw` matters

A browser DAW can easily become dependent on a hosted account or a remote database. DAWWW-CORE takes a different approach: the active project can live locally, while `.dw` acts as a portable copy that can be archived, transferred and reopened.

That makes cross-device continuity independent from a mandatory synchronization service. Future online services can make transfer easier, but **they are not what makes Desktop and Android compatible**.

## What follows the project

The cross-device model is intended to preserve the parts that make a DAW session actually reusable rather than merely opening a simplified representation:

- song structure and arrangement;
- sequencer patterns and detailed step data;
- piano-roll note data;
- selected instruments and their settings;
- effects and processing state;
- automation;
- routing and mixer state represented by the project;
- referenced sampler content required by the session.

The aim is straightforward: opening the project on the other platform should mean **continuing the project**, not rebuilding it.

## Local-first, cross-device

Cross-device and local-first are not opposites here.

Desktop keeps working projects locally and can export them as `.dw`. The same portable project is the foundation used by Android. This preserves a clear local copy even when cloud services are unavailable or simply unwanted.

Important projects can therefore be backed up outside a browser profile and moved without depending on a permanently hosted copy.

## Platform differences do not create a second project

Desktop and Android naturally have different screen sizes, input methods and distribution models. Their interfaces can adapt to those differences without creating a second project format.

The commercial model is also different:

- **Desktop Web:** available now without payment;
- **Android:** upcoming subscription version.

That difference affects access to the platform, not the creative project itself.

> **One DAWWW-CORE project. Desktop when you want the full large-screen workspace, Android when you want to continue on mobile.**
